About Argos Monitor

Argos Monitor tracks news and crisis reporting across 99 countries, placing domestic press, local-language diaspora outlets, and international coverage side by side. Every headline and quote links directly to the original article; nothing is paraphrased or written by an LLM.

Core principles

  1. No statement without a source: Every headline and quote is copied verbatim from a stored article and linked back to it. Nothing is summarised or invented.
  2. No LLM in the pipeline: Stories are grouped, sorted, and ranked with multilingual text matching and transparent rules rather than generative AI.
  3. Reprints don’t count twice: Identical wire copies and syndicated reprints are collapsed into one, so 20 reprints count as a single independent outlet.
  4. Domestic, diaspora, and international side by side: Each story shows where coverage comes from, with clear labels separating official state outlets from independent reporting.
  5. Elections and crises first: National elections, referendums, active conflict, and major disasters receive dedicated tracking and priority placement.

How it works

Argos Monitor runs unattended on a fixed schedule: full digests with article extraction and offline headline translation run twice daily, fast feed snapshots update stories every 4 hours, and live crisis alerts refresh every 2 hours around the clock.

01 · CollectPolls 2,494 news feeds across 99 country desks alongside live conflict, disaster, and travel-advisory monitors, filtering out sports, routine weather forecasts, horoscopes, and lifestyle noise.
02 · Group & deduplicateClusters articles covering the same event across languages, collapses syndicated wire reprints, and counts distinct independent outlets.
03 · Sort & verifySorts stories into six policy areas, elevates elections and fast-rising coverage, and extracts verbatim quotes verified character-for-character against the source article.
04 · Translate & publishPre-translates top non-English headlines in 15 core languages during twice-daily digests (with browser translation for other languages and breaking items) and publishes static briefings with no tracking.

Labels & methodology

Expand any section below for details on source classifications, story signals, crisis feeds, and translation.

Source alignment, editorial leaning & official labels
Ownership & alignment Outlets are classified as State-controlled (statutory agencies and ministry organs), Pro-government (private media aligned with the ruling coalition), Public broadcaster (chartered public-service media), Independent (free of state ownership), Opposition (dissident, diaspora, or exile press), or party-aligned where relevant (such as Pro-Fidesz in Hungary).
Editorial leaning & factuality Leaning (Left to Right, or State-aligned in state-directed systems) and factuality (Very High to Mixed) are calibrated against Media Bias/Fact Check, AllSides, and Reuters Institute benchmarks.
Story corroboration labels Story cards flag state-dominated coverage so readers can tell official messaging from independent reporting at a glance:
Official source At least one statutory government or state-wire outlet covers the story
Mostly state media 70% or more of corroborating coverage comes from state-controlled or pro-government outlets
Verbatim quotes & reprints Pull-quotes are checked directly against stored article text; any mismatch is dropped. Near-identical wire reprints (such as VNA, dpa, APA, PAP, MTI, or Yonhap) are collapsed so they do not inflate outlet counts.
Story ranking, coverage shifts & briefings
How stories are ranked Stories are ordered by independent outlet count, cross-scope corroboration (domestic, diaspora, and international), recency, and policy relevance. Elections receive top priority; routine crime, traffic, and celebrity items are down-weighted.
Coverage velocity & pickups Compares each story against the preceding equal window (24h or 7d) to flag unusual acceleration or international attention:
Surging (+N)Sharp rise in coverage compared with the prior period
Intl pickupDomestic story picked up by international wires
New today · Cooling · Picked up abroadShown inside expanded story details alongside state-coverage shifts
Six policy domains & Desk balance Stories are sorted into Politics & Governance, Economy & Trade, Diplomacy & Foreign Policy, Defence & Security (with a defence-industry focus filter), Society & Rights, and Other. Each country desk includes a Desk balance panel comparing today’s topic share against its 7-day baseline.
Overview & Country Brief export The Overview opens on Today’s Briefing (eight thematic blocks with one country per slot and expandable sub-stories) alongside the searchable World Directory. Every Country Brief includes an inline Export brief control for A4 Print / PDF, Markdown copy, or download.
Crisis, conflict & auxiliary signals
2-hourly live alerts Refreshes around the clock from GDELT conflict events, UN Peace & Security, Crisis Group, HRW, LWJ, and MSF field reports, US State Dept / UK FCDO travel advisories, and GDACS / USGS / NASA EONET hazard feeds.
Conflict & religious sensitivity tracks Separates active violence into Armed Clash & Strike, Insurgency & Terror, and Civil Unrest & Crackdown. Tags Religious sensitivity only when a faith community or sacred site co-occurs with active violence or coercion.
Auxiliary context (unverified) Public Telegram channel previews, regional Reddit discussions, and Google Trends surges appear in a separate drawer clearly marked unverified and never mix into verified story clusters or outlet counts.
Translation & privacy
Offline headline translation (15 languages) During twice-daily digests, top non-English headlines in 15 languages (Arabic, Croatian, Czech, French, German, Hungarian, Indonesian, Italian, Polish, Russian, Slovak, Slovenian, Spanish, Ukrainian, and Vietnamese) are pre-translated offline using open-source models and entity glossaries for personal and place names.
Browser translation & originals Breaking stories between daily digests, other languages, and full quotes can be translated on-device in your browser. The original-language article always remains the authoritative source and is one click away.
Zero tracking Delivered as static HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript on Cloudflare Pages. No cookies, accounts, or analytics; outbound links strip referrer headers.
